SPEED.Compact

Designing the operating system for the new company oven

The real challenge? Designing a brand-new operating system by reusing the foundation of an old product from 2018, originally built for a completely different user.

After diving into user research to understand this new target, we carefully figured out what was worth keeping, what needed a serious upgrade, and what could quietly disappear.

All of this, with a software budget so tight it required plenty of smart decisions.
In the end, we cooked up an interface that’s simple, efficient, and exactly what users needed—no extra sauce.

Problem

How might create a fresh and user-friendly product using the constraints of a legacy system originally designed for a different audience and a limited software budget??

Outcome

A clean UX designed by optimizing existing flows and avoiding unnecessary UI complexity, tailored to a new audience without exceeding the limits.

Interviews with Sales and Dealers

We kicked things off the old-fashioned way: by talking to people who actually know stuff.
Through conversations with our multi-brand dealers and in-house experts (whose past lives gave them deep insight into this brand-new market segment), we ran semi-structured interviews with open-ended questions to define:

  • our real user base
  • their standard workflows
  • key needs and frustrations
  • and which existing products meet their expectations

Challenge

Different stakeholders described different user profiles, sometimes with conflicting needs and priorities. Some emphasized speed and simplicity, while others pointed to more experienced operators needing advanced control.

This raised an essential question: Are we building one product for multiple users—or trying to please everyone and risking pleasing no one?

Competitor Analysis & Observational Research

You can always learn from the best, and sometimes even from the rest.
As a leading brand in other foodservice sectors, we took a humble approach and analyzed our competitors in depth.

To find clarity, we ran passive observation sessions in selected chains and bars. No forms, no questions, just eyes wide open and a lot of silent notes.

The goal? To figure out what users love, what they tolerate, and where competitors fall short—so we could hit the sweet spot they’ve been missing.

Analysis of the Existing Product

Before reinventing the wheel, we mapped it. We created a full user flow of the existing operating system to understand what we were dealing with.

Spoiler alert: there was a lot we didn’t need.

We trimmed the fat, simplified the experience, and started aligning the system with what our actual users needed, not what a 2018 spec sheet once said.

Differentiated program flows

We separated the experience between library and favorite programs. From the library, tapping a recipe opens a detailed view where users can see or edit parameters (control). From the favorites, tapping a recipe launches it directly(speed).

Custom roles & interface visibility

A new permission system allows managers to tailor the UI by hiding irrelevant sections, giving users focus and clarity (control). For chains, the home screen can be limited to just two areas: Favorites and Manual Cleaning, keeping things ultra-simple.

SpeedUnox Library

A dedicated section featuring a collection of Unox-developed, fully tested recipes. Users can either save them to their personal recipe list or simply explore their parameters for customization and use.

This provides a reliable starting point, especially for new users or chains looking to standardize quality without starting from scratch.

Guided cleaning procedure

In collaboration with our tech team, we designed and tested a guided cleaning workflow.

The oven notifies the user when cleaning is due, then walks them through the process step by step.

Completed cleanings are tracked and viewable via UNOX Cloud by area managers.

SMART.Energy technology

We developed an algorithm that maximizes oven performance during peak service and automatically reduces energy use and costs during quieter hours, helping kitchens stay efficient without compromise.
